## 3 — Unwinding enclosures (theory-of-change block)

**Heading:** Unwinding enclosures

> For three centuries, enclosure has fenced off what was once held in common — first land, then housing, and increasingly the value communities create together. We work in the other direction. Every project is a small act of unwinding: returning a parcel, a building, a flow of value to collective hands — and building the legal, economic and cultural scaffolding that lets it stay there.

## 4 — How we work (replaces "Our Approach")

**Heading:** How we work

**Intro:**
> We start in place, not with a solution. We read a context across three registers — legal, cultural and economic — and look for the rare moment where all three align. Where they do, an enclosure can be unwound: the alignment is the opening, and the mechanism we build is how we hold it open.

### The three registers (reframed cards)

**Where the money can move differently** *(was: Inverting Civic Wealth)*
> We find the economic strategy that already wants to work — a pooled fund, a community-led exit, value that could recirculate locally instead of leaking out — and design the mechanism that lets it.

**Where the law gives** *(was: Modernising Agreements)*
> We find the opening in the law — an underused right, a route to collective tenure, an edge others read as a dead end — and build the agreements that turn it into durable, shared ownership.

**Where the community is ready** *(was: Cultivating Playful Rituals)*
> We find the cultural predisposition — customary practice, trust, the willingness to act together — and design the rituals and interfaces that turn readiness into coordinated action.

**Technology — the connective layer** *(conditional, not a fourth pillar)*
> When a mechanism has to run across many people or places, we build the software — registries, pledge pools, coordination tools — to make it operate. Never the starting point; always in service of the unlock.

---

## 5 — Worked example (the approach in one move)

**Threshold pools**

| Register | What we found |
|---|---|
| **Legal** | Striking on rent is illegal in Germany. |
| **Social** | Tenants will only pledge if it's anonymous and collective. |
| **Economic** | A pledge that triggers only at critical mass makes the risk bearable. |

> So we built **threshold pools**: anonymous, collective pledging that activates only once enough neighbours commit. A legal constraint, a social need and an economic insight — unlocked by a single tool.
