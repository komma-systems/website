We don't build tools for their own sake. We study a place, find what keeps land and housing locked up — an opening in the law, a cultural readiness, an economic strategy that works — and unlock the means for communities to own them in common.

Komma is a venture collective that uses applied research, artistic inquiry and real-world demonstration to shift how we value, own and care for what is held in common.

We're architects, researchers and economists who work from place — partnering with communities, municipalities, philanthropy and the private sector to find where land and housing stay locked in speculation, and unlock the legal, cultural and economic means to hold them in common. We build technology only when it's needed to make that work at scale.

Unwinding enclosures

For three centuries, enclosure has fenced off what was once held in common — first land, then housing, then the value communities create together. We work the other way: every project is a small act of unwinding, and the legal, economic and cultural scaffolding that lets it stay unwound.

How we work

We start in place, not with a solution. We read a context across three registers and look for the rare moment where all three align. That alignment is the opening — the mechanism we build is how we hold it open.

Legal

Where the law gives

We find the opening in the law — an underused right, a route to collective tenure, an edge others read as a dead end — and build the agreements that turn it into durable, shared ownership.

Economic

Where the money can move differently

We find the economic strategy that already wants to work — a pooled fund, a community-led exit, value that recirculates locally instead of leaking out — and design the mechanism that lets it.

Cultural · Social

Where the community is ready

We find the cultural predisposition — customary practice, trust, the willingness to act together — and design the rituals and interfaces that turn readiness into coordinated action.

Technology · the connective layer

Built only when a mechanism must run across many people or places — registries, pledge pools, coordination tools. In service of the unlock, never the start.

The approach in one move
Threshold pools
LegalStriking on rent is illegal in Germany.
SocialTenants will only pledge if it's anonymous and collective.
EconomicA pledge that triggers only at critical mass makes the risk bearable.

→ So we built threshold pools: anonymous, collective pledging that activates only once enough neighbours commit. A legal constraint, a social need and an economic insight — unlocked by a single tool.
